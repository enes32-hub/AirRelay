1. Summary

  • AirRelay Receiver does not collect, transmit or sell your personal data. There is no account, no sign-in and no developer server.
  • The app works entirely on your own local network, between your Apple device (iPhone, iPad or Mac) and your Android TV.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, no crash-reporting SDKs are included.
  • Anything the app stores — screen recordings, technical logs, settings — is stored on your device and stays there unless you choose to share it.
  • The app declares the microphone-adjacent permission RECORD_AUDIO, but it never reads your microphone. That permission is the technical prerequisite of the Android API used to capture the app's own audio while you record. See section 5.

2. What the app does

AirRelay Receiver turns an Android TV device into an AirPlay receiver. When you start screen mirroring from an Apple device on the same local network, the TV shows that screen and plays its audio. When an app offers AirPlay video, the TV can play that video directly by fetching the address the sending device hands over.

On request, the app can also:

  • record the incoming picture and sound to a video file stored on the TV,
  • keep technical logs to help diagnose problems.

All of this happens on the TV. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

6. Network activity

  • The app communicates only on your local network (LAN). It publishes Bonjour/mDNS records (_airplay._tcp and _raop._tcp) so your device can find the TV, and it listens for the AirPlay session on local ports (7000 by default for screen mirroring, plus the ports negotiated for each session).
  • The app contains no hard-coded internet address and contacts no developer or third-party service. There is no telemetry endpoint to send anything to.
  • Direct video playback: when a sending app offers video playback, your Apple device hands the TV a media address; the TV player then fetches that stream from wherever the app hosts it. This is the content you asked to watch, requested by the TV at your device's instruction — it is not a connection to the developer.
  • Encryption between your Apple device and the TV is negotiated by the AirPlay protocol itself. This app does not add a separate layer on top, does not redirect the traffic anywhere else, and therefore only sees the traffic that the protocol delivers to it.
  • Android's cleartext-traffic flag is enabled for local protocol compatibility, so the app can accept unencrypted local AirPlay traffic. It is not used to reach the internet.

8. Third-party software and open-source components

The app includes open-source components, among them:

  • UxPlay, playfair/RPiPlay and android-airplay-server (GPL-3.0) — AirPlay receiver implementation,
  • FFmpeg (LGPL-2.1-or-later, minimal ALAC-only build) — audio decoding,
  • libplist and libcnary (LGPL-2.1-or-later) — property-list parsing,
  • OpenSSL (Apache-2.0) — cryptography,
  • AndroidX, Jetpack Compose, Media3 (ExoPlayer), Material, Dagger/Hilt, Oboe, Kotlin and kotlinx.coroutines (Apache-2.0),
  • llhttp, csrp, jsmn, time64 and openssl-cmake (MIT), TargetArch.cmake (BSD-2-Clause).

The complete licence texts and copyright notices are available inside the app, under Settings → About → Component licences. No advertising, analytics, attribution or tracking SDK from any third party is included.
